1.What DEDUCT does

A collection of related genomes repeats most of its content across records. General-purpose coders and single-sequence DNA coders compress each record on its own, so a collection of near-identical strains costs close to the single-genome entropy of about 1.9 bits per base. DEDUCT makes the shared structure explicit before coding, and then codes the structure.

The front end is alignment-aware. It normalizes each assembly, separates the contigs of every assembly into contig columns (the n-th contig of each assembly forms one column), clusters each column with an FFT cross-correlation that is invariant to rotation and to strand, and aligns the members of each cluster to a consensus with a gap-aware global alignment. Rotation, strand, and insertions are recorded as invertible parameters. No external reference is used.

Each aligned matrix is compressed by a trained coder: a convolutional autoencoder whose bottleneck is a vertical block discrete cosine transform (DCT), applied along the inter-genome axis. After alignment, a column of the matrix is one locus sampled across genomes, so the DCT concentrates the signal on a few low-frequency coefficients. This route is the learned path. The compressed output holds the quantized latent, an argmax residual (the true symbol at every position where the decoder's prediction differs from the original), and the decoder weights. Sequences that join no cluster go to a global fallback: a length-ordered pool compressed with gzip, whose DEFLATE algorithm finds repeats within a 32 KiB window.

The method is constructively lossless. The residual is computed against the dequantized latent, the same input the decoder receives, and a margin rule writes a residual entry at every position whose top two logits are closer than a margin, so no arithmetic difference below half that margin can change a decoded symbol. A weaker model costs bytes, not correctness. Every reported bitrate counts the model weights inside the compressed output, and every compressed collection was decoded in an isolated process and compared with the input file by file.

  • 0.3755 F-BPB on 11.22 GB of E. coli / Shigella assemblies (ecoli1): a 21.2× ratio and a 95.28% space saving
  • 0.0259 D-BPB of the trained coder on the 48.7% of ecoli1 it codes, all 774 decoder networks included
  • 0.0180 F-BPB across 67,190 human mitochondrial genomes, a 337.9× ratio
  • 362,645 files decoded and checked against their SHA-256 digests across the paper's corpora, with zero failures
  • 613 parameters in each stored decoder, 1,175 to 1,220 B per cluster in the compressed output

ecoli1: 2,397 assemblies, 1,016,248 records, 1.108 × 1010 nucleotides, drawn from NCBI Pathogen Detection.

2.The pipeline and the codec

An orthogonal transform concentrates energy only if the axis along which it operates is coherent. Along a single genome it is not. Across a stack of aligned homologous genomes it is: each aligned column is a single locus, dominated by the consensus base plus sparse single-nucleotide deviations. Alignment supplies the coherence, and the transform runs along the inter-genome axis where the signal is highly similar.

The three front-end steps each solve a concrete problem. Contig separation groups the n-th record of every assembly into one column, so that homologous material is compared with homologous material. FFT cross-correlation recovers clusters although assemblers pick different linear start points on a circular replicon and may emit either strand. A target joins a cluster when its best match fraction is at least 0.6, and at most 30 clusters are seeded per column. Gap-aware alignment to a consensus built from the members at the cluster's most common length absorbs insertions and deletions; a member is accepted if its edit distance is at most 0.35 times the consensus length. Members that exceed this bound, clusters with fewer than 64 members, and clusters narrower than one full tile of 512 columns are routed to the fallback.

Two convolutions with a 3 × 1 kernel and a 2 × 1 stride downsample the inter-genome axis and leave the sequence axis untouched; the kernel is one column wide so that the latent transports one column rather than a mixture of neighbors. The block DCT runs down the inter-genome axis, and a learnable soft threshold follows it. On the coding path the latent is quantized with a collection-wide step s = 16, and the quantizer's dead zone, two orders of magnitude above the learned threshold, accounts for more than 99.9% of the zeroed coefficients. Only the decoder half is stored: 613 parameters in float16 per cluster, with the DCT matrix regenerated from the block height. The prediction used for the residual is computed from the dequantized latent with the rounded weights, so the residual is taken against the exact network that runs during decoding. One model is trained from scratch per cluster with Adam at a learning rate of 5 × 10−3; every fifth epoch the compressed size is measured, and the weights kept are those that gave the smallest size.

3.Results

Two bitrates are reported for every collection. D-BPB is the trained coder's cost over the bases it received: weights, latent, residual, and alignment metadata, divided by the bases of the learned path. F-BPB is what the entire pipeline costs over the entire collection, the fallback and the ambiguity exceptions included. Neither rate includes the side channel (the FASTA headers, the record order, and the contig membership), whose size scales with the number of records rather than with the number of bases. The compression ratio does include it: it is the normalized input bytes divided by the compressed output on disk, every byte counted. Coverage, the share of the called bases that reached the trained coder, is what makes F-BPB interpretable: at low coverage it measures the fallback rather than the learned path.

On ecoli1, the largest and most fragmented input, the whole pipeline reaches 0.3755 F-BPB and a 21.2× ratio while the trained coder reaches 0.0259 D-BPB on the 48.7% of the corpus it codes; the mitochondrial and SARS-CoV-2 collections compress at 129 to 338×, and DNACorpus, where no cluster can form, marks the boundary of the operating regime.

On ecoli1 the front end recovers 817 clusters across 612 contig columns, and the learned path codes 774 of them, spanning 5.40 × 109 aligned bases. The largest single cluster spans 1,205 of the 2,397 genomes. Averaged over all 774 clusters, the argmax accuracy before residual correction is 97.5%; the seven largest clusters average 99.2% and the 163 clusters admitted just above the admission threshold average 79.8%. Scaling the mitochondrial collection from 1,000 to 67,190 records improves the trained coder's rate from 0.0252 to 0.0163 D-BPB, because the same decoder is amortized over sixty-seven times as many sequences.

The side channel scales with the number of records rather than the number of bases, so it is 24.2% of the mitochondrial compressed output but 1.6% of ecoli1's; on ecoli1 the latent is the largest learned-path term, and on the other three coded collections the residual is.

The operating regime, measured on SARS-CoV-2

The SARS-CoV-2 collection is 291,017 complete genomes, the total of a per-year quota over 2020 to 2026: 50,000 records where a publication year has that many, and every record available at the fetch date where it does not. Each year is compressed as its own collection, trained only on its own data.

The coverage column tracks the ambiguity content of each year's consensus genomes rather than time: ambiguity content runs 0.05%, 0.06%, 2.72%, 3.95%, 0.66%, 4.05% and 0.89% for 2020 through 2026, a Spearman ρ of −0.96 and a Pearson r of −0.99 against that column.

The cause is the separation step, which removes non-ACGT characters before clustering; each removal shifts every later coordinate, so a record with many uncalled bases can no longer be rotation-aligned with its relatives. Such records are set aside because of missing data rather than sequence divergence. The learned path's advantage shrinks gradually as coverage falls: the rate of the trained coder is 13.2× below that of the pool where it receives 98.2% of the bases (2020) and 8.7× below it where it receives 76.1% (2025); on S. enterica, at 21.7% coverage, its rate is still 3.3× below that of the pool.

4.What the ablation shows

Replacing the DCT with the identity, with nothing else changed, drops the argmax accuracy from 0.988 to 0.433 and increases the compressed size by a factor of 201.7; the transform amplifies the preprocessing stage rather than acting as an independent source of gain. The contig-column decomposition is evaluated on the full E. coli corpus: clustering within columns delivers 2.2× as much of the corpus to the learned path as clustering a column-free pool. The two coding paths are complementary. Routing the learned path's own input through the fallback pool costs 87× its rate, because in the pool each sequence's nearest copy lies far outside DEFLATE's search window, while the sequences the front end declines cannot be aligned at all, so only the fallback can code them. A single shared model across clusters is ruled out: a model trained on one cluster and applied frozen to another predicts at the level expected by chance, so the compressed output carries one decoder per cluster. What sharing would provide is training time, since a warm-started cluster converges in 3.5× fewer epochs.

5.Model-inclusive accounting

A decoder that re-executes a trained network needs that network, so the weights are part of the compressed representation. DEDUCT counts them in every reported size. The class of learnable lossless coders has no shared benchmark; mutually comparable rates exist only as the re-runs of Sun et al., in which every coder ran under one protocol on one machine, and there the previous learnable coders operate at 1.845 to 1.930 BPB. Where a static model is used, its bytes are usually excluded. For DNA-BiLSTM the effect has been quantified in print: its mean rate rises from 1.907 BPB with the model excluded to 6.991 BPB with it included.

Because PMKLC's checkpoint is public, the same rule can be applied to it. Counting the 2,070,216 B static model once per dataset on PMKLC's own nine-dataset benchmark raises PMKLC-S's reported mean from 1.845 to 2.724 BPB. Two qualifications bound that mean. The penalty falls as 1/N and is driven by the small files: +2.65 BPB on the 6.25 MB dataset against +0.016 BPB on the 1.05 GB one. It is a mean over datasets; coded as one collection, the nine would have paid +0.008 BPB for the same model.

Direct comparison on identical input

PMKLC-S was run with its released code and static public model on the same 16,568,048 bases that DEDUCT codes for the 1,000-record mitochondrial subset. It reached 0.0703 BPB over those bases, and 1.0700 BPB once the model is counted as this paper's protocol requires. DEDUCT's model-inclusive rate on the same input is 0.0255 F-BPB, with all 1,000 files byte-exact.

The decompression of PMKLC-S could not be verified: in the authors' environment, the released decompressor returned a file of the correct length whose contents diverge from the input after 121 bytes and differ in 74% of the file, on two datasets, with and without TF32. The paper does not conclude that the method is lossy; the fault may lie in the environment, and the compressed size is reported as unconfirmed.

The protocol penalizes DEDUCT more than the previous coders: their models are fixed and amortize over any corpus, whereas DEDUCT's decoders grow with the cluster count and amortize over none. The weight term is 0.18% of the ecoli1 compressed output. To our knowledge, no previous learnable, reference-free, byte-exact method exploits cross-genome redundancy at the collection scale, and none establishes a model-inclusive bitrate below 1 BPB.

6.Throughput and memory

Compression carries the training epochs and decompression does not. On ecoli1 (11.22 GB), compression takes 7,811.5 s in total, of which 734.4 s is clustering, 6,181.3 s the overlapped alignment-and-coding span (model training is 6,174.0 s of it, 7.98 s per cluster over the 774 coded clusters), and 875.4 s the fallback. Decompression with a per-file SHA-256 check in an isolated process takes 210.2 s. That is 1.44 MB/s in and 53.4 MB/s out. Across the learned-path corpora, decompression is 2.7 to 58× faster than compression, and 138× on DNACorpus, where nothing is trained.

All experiments use a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPU (32 GB), an AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D CPU, and 64 GB of DDR5 RAM. GPU memory peaks during clustering, which holds the Fourier transforms of every sequence in the largest contig column in memory at once: 22.1 GB on ecoli1 and 26.6 GB on S. enterica, the highest observed. Compression itself peaks near 11 GB, because only one cluster's model is in memory at a time. The clustering cache is limited by free VRAM and falls back to recomputation, so the pipeline runs on GPUs with less memory, at lower speed.

The repository will contain:

  • The implementation: the front end (normalization, contig separation, FFT clustering, gap-aware alignment), the trained coder with its argmax residual, the global fallback, and the side channel, behind the deduct command line.
  • The configuration: the proposed configuration, fixed once and used unchanged for every corpus in the paper, with no per-dataset tuning.
  • The fetch script and the accession lists: every collection except DNACorpus is public NCBI data, and the lists with the fetch script rebuild each corpus and check it against a recorded digest.
  • The provenance file: records the fetch dates, including the mid-2026 date up to which the 2026 SARS-CoV-2 records were taken.
  • The test suite (52 tests covering the coders, the front end, the exceptions blob, and an end-to-end round trip).

Corpus sizes. ecoli1 is 11.22 GB (2,397 assemblies); S. enterica is 5.20 GB as downloaded and 1.19% smaller after normalization (1,024 genomes); the mitochondrial collection is 1.12 GB (67,190 records) and its 1,000-record subset 16.7 MB; the SARS-CoV-2 years are 0.95, 1.50, 1.50, 1.50, 1.49, 1.50 and 0.28 GB for 2020 through 2026 (291,017 genomes in total); DNACorpus is 0.69 GB (17 files) and should be obtained from its authors' distribution.

8.Reproducing a result

Every corpus in the paper is compressed by the same command in the default configuration, differing only in the input directory. Verification decompresses in an isolated process and compares each reconstructed file against the SHA-256 digest taken at separation time.

# compress a corpus directory into a compressed output. --checksums stores a
# SHA-256 digest per input file; digest storage is off by default.
deduct compress <corpus> <out> --checksums

# decompress in an isolated process and compare every file with its digest.
deduct verify <out>

Every compressed output behind the tables was written with digests, and the reported sizes subtract the stored digest bytes back out exactly, because the digests are a separable side-channel term; on the mitochondrial collection they are roughly half of the compressed output, 6.03 MB against 3.31 MB without them. Over the 362,645 files of the paper, verification reports zero failures. All runs fix the random seeds, and both the encoder and the decoder restrict cuDNN to deterministic algorithms and disable TF32; the margin rule of Section 1 makes the decoded symbols independent of the arithmetic below half the margin.

Reconstruction is exact with respect to the normalized form of the input: wrapped sequence lines joined into one line per record, sequences made upper-case, and each record's index appended to its header. Upper-casing is the one deliberately lossy step, since soft-masking annotations are discarded. The learning rate is the only hyper-parameter that can vary per corpus, and it is 5 × 10−3 for every corpus reported.
